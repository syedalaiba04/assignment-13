3. A confused classmate's question
Classmate: I don't understand why we calculate both mean and median. Isn't the mean enough to tell us the average price?

Me: I thought that at first too, but they don't always tell us exactly the same thing. The mean is calculated by adding all the prices and dividing by the number of prices. The median is the middle value when the prices are arranged in order.

Classmate: But if both are averages, why do we need two?

Me: Because unusual values can affect the mean a lot. For example, Sweetener D has a price of 9999 in the dataset. If we include that value, the mean for Week 2 becomes 576.81, but without it, the mean is 167.15. The median changes much less, from 167.75 to 167.50.

Classmate: So does that mean the median is always better?

Me: Not always. It depends on the data. The mean uses all the values, while the median is less affected by extreme values. That's why it is useful to look at both instead of choosing one without checking.
